\begin{abstract}
The cross-entropy method (CEM) planner used with LeWorldModel (LeWM) ranks candidate action
sequences by the Euclidean distance between a predicted terminal latent and a goal latent. While
this objective succeeds on short-horizon goals, its performance degrades sharply as goals become
more temporally distant or come from a different episode. On Push-T, the planner reaches
same-episode goals 25, 50 and 100 steps ahead, and cross-episode goals, with success rates of
91/41/13/14\%, respectively. We address this degradation at long horizons and across episodes
without retraining the world model or learning a subgoal generator. We learn a temporal
distance representation over frozen latents, build a graph of the offline dataset, and select a subgoal along the graph's shortest path. A reachability critic scores predicted endpoints by expected time to goal, and latent L2 is used for the final horizon. Each component addresses a distinct limitation: the subgoal provides \emph{direction}, the critic accounts for \emph{reachability}, and the switch to L2 restores near-goal \emph{resolution}. The graph finds a route for every evaluated cross-episode pair, but reaching the goal still requires the critic.
Across goals at offsets 50 and 100, and on cross-episode goals, our method raises success from 41/13/14\% to 70/46/42\% on Push-T,
from 91/83/29\% to 98/98/66\% on Reacher, and from 34/36/16\% to 50/50/28\% on Cube. At the 25-step horizon, our method trails L2
by 2.8 points on Push-T (88.4 vs 91.2) and 1.2 points on Reacher (76.0 vs 77.2), but leads by 6.0 points on Cube (62.0 vs 56.0).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% \keypoints{
% \item The problem is the \emph{objective}, not the model: terminal latent L2 stops ranking
%       candidates past $\sim$50 steps and plateaus on cross-episode goals at 14\% even with a
%       5$\times$ larger budget.
% \item Existing fixes retrain the encoder, add predictors, or learn generators. We keep the
%       world model and its planner frozen and use the dataset itself as a graph.
% \item Three mechanisms, three failures: direction (graph subgoal), reachability (hitting-time
%       critic), resolution (final switch). Neither subgoal nor critic alone moves cross-episode.
% \item Cross-episode goal reaching is the headline evaluation; same-episode long horizons are
%       supporting evidence.
% \item Contributions list, four items, each tied to a table or figure.
% }

Joint-embedding world models trained from pixels \citep{lewm,dinowm,pldm} plan by search: they
sample candidate action sequences, roll each through a learned latent predictor, and select the
sequence whose predicted terminal latent lies closest to the goal latent in Euclidean (L2)
distance -- the default objective we call L2 throughout. On the horizons these
models are typically evaluated on, where the goal is a frame from the same trajectory a fixed
number of steps ahead, this objective performs well. Its performance degrades sharply, however,
as the goal becomes more temporally distant. With the frozen LeWorldModel (LeWM) checkpoint and
its published cross-entropy-method (CEM) planner, Push-T success falls from 91\% for goals
25 steps ahead to 13\% at 100 steps and 14\% for goals drawn from a different episode. The failure
is not a budget constraint: a 250-step budget instead of 100 adds barely two points
(\textcolor{red}{\cref{tab:diagnostics}b}). In \cref{sec:problem} we show that it lies in the
objective itself, not just in the predictor.

A substantial body of recent work targets the same failure in the same frozen-model setting,
almost uniformly by learning something new on top of it: a subgoal generator and action prior
\citep{sage}, a macro-action space and high-level predictor \citep{hilewm}, world models operating
at several time scales \citep{hwm}, an action-free latent planner \citep{ffjepa}, or a retrained
encoder whose geometry directly encodes temporal cost \citep{tdjepa,rcaux,trm}. A separate, older
line of work in offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning addresses long horizons by
searching over the data directly: it builds a graph over the offline dataset and lets shortest
paths select subgoals \citep{sptm,sorb,sgm,gas,ttgs,alps}, handing the resulting subgoal to a
learned goal-conditioned policy. To our knowledge, no prior method places the dataset graph inside
a pixel world model's planner itself. \citet{hilewm} point to why that would help: from their own
hierarchical planner, they identify the conditions under which a subgoal interface transfers to a
compact frozen world model -- subgoals must remain ``data-supported, executable, and matched to
the temporal scale.''

We place the dataset graph inside LeWM's planner: a subgoal that is data-supported,
executable, and matched to the temporal scale by construction. We learn a temporal distance
representation (TDR)
on the frozen LeWM latents \citep{hilp,gas}, build a graph over the offline dataset in that
space, and replace LeWM's terminal cost with the distance to a subgoal -- a dataset frame reached
from the current state by the shortest path of observed transitions toward the goal, truncated to
one planning horizon. This alone meaningfully improves the mid-range regime, but does nothing for
cross-episode goals: the graph finds a route for every pair we evaluate, well inside the planning
budget, yet the planner does not execute it. The missing component is reachability, which we
address with a critic $V(\z,\z_g,h)$, the probability that the goal is reachable from latent $\z$
within $h$ steps, and score each predicted endpoint by \emph{expected hitting time} under the
steps remaining. With this term, and with the final horizon handed
back to LeWM's own objective once the goal is within one horizon, a single configuration matches or
exceeds every alternative we tested at offsets 50 and above and on cross-episode goals, across
three environments -- Push-T, Reacher, and Cube (\cref{tab:main}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item A planner-side method for long-horizon and cross-episode goal reaching with a frozen
        pixel world model, requiring no new generator, policy, or predictor: a TDR on frozen
        latents, a dataset graph, and a hitting-time critic, all scored through LeWM's own CEM
        planner without modification (\cref{sec:method}).
  \item A budget-clocked expected-hitting-time cost that replaces the \textcolor{red}{standard}
        $-\log V$ feasibility term, removes its dependence on a weighting hyperparameter, and
        preserves a progress gradient among feasible candidates (\cref{sec:critic,sec:ablation}).
  \item \textcolor{red}{The first} cross-episode goal-reaching evaluation for latent MPC on
        Push-T, Reacher, and Cube (\cref{sec:setup,tab:main}).
  \item A \textcolor{red}{mechanistic account}, supported by per-pair diagnostics, of which failure mode each
        component addresses: direction, reachability, and resolution
        (\cref{sec:analysis}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% \keypoints{
% \item Group by what is learned, not by year: (i) planner-side costs on a frozen model,
%       (ii) hierarchical planning with learned generators/predictors, (iii) graph search over
%       offline data with learned policies, (iv) value functions inside MPC.
% \item For each group say in one clause what we take and in one clause what we do not.
% \item Be explicit that SAGE reports higher same-episode numbers at $H\!=\!150$ and cannot stitch
%       by construction; do not hide it, it is our argument.
% }

\paragraph{Planning with latent world models.}
PlaNet and Dreamer \citep{planet,dreamerv3} plan or learn policies in a reconstruction-based
latent; DINO-WM \citep{dinowm}, PLDM \citep{pldm}, V-JEPA~2-AC \citep{vjepa2} and LeWM
\citep{lewm} plan with zero-shot CEM in a joint-embedding latent, scoring candidates by terminal
latent distance. \citet{jepawm-study} and \citet{stableworldmodel} study which design choices
matter; the planning horizon is a known weak point of all of them.

\paragraph{Repairing the cost on a frozen model.}
Trajectory reachability metrics \citep{trm} learn a pairwise temporal cost from logged
trajectories and use it as the terminal ranking; temporal-distance JEPA \citep{tdjepa},
RC-aux \citep{rcaux}, DA-LeWM \citep{dalewm} and SCALE \citep{scale} push similar signals into
the encoder at training time, and \citet{valueguided,straightening} shape the geometry toward a
value or a straight path. Our TDR is in the same family (an expectile temporal distance on frozen
latents) but we do not use it as the cost: we use it to build a graph and pick a data-supported
target, and we show the metric alone is not what helps (\cref{sec:ablation}). RC-aux's
budget-conditioned reachability is the closest relative of our critic; ours is a separate
network with an explicit plan clock and never touches the encoder.

\paragraph{Hierarchical planning with world models.}
HWM \citep{hwm} learns world models at several temporal scales and uses long-horizon predictions
as subgoals; SAGE \citep{sage} learns a subgoal generator and a subgoal-conditioned action prior
that CEM refines; FF-JEPA \citep{ffjepa} learns an action-free latent planner; Hi-LeWM
\citep{hilewm} learns macro-actions and a high-level predictor and shows the naive version loses
to flat planning because CEM exploits the learned subgoal space. Each of these trains a generative
component whose training pairs are same-trajectory windows, so their subgoals cannot lie on a
route that no single trajectory took. Our subgoals are dataset frames on a shortest path over
the whole dataset, and the cross-episode protocol is where that difference shows. SAGE reports
stronger same-episode numbers than ours at long offsets (64.7\% at $H\!=\!150$ on Push-T with the
same frozen model); we discuss this in \cref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Graph search over offline data.}
SPTM \citep{sptm}, SoRB \citep{sorb} and SGM \citep{sgm} build graphs over replay buffers with a
learned distance and plan waypoints for a goal-conditioned policy; GAS \citep{gas} clusters a
temporal distance representation \citep{hilp,qrl} into nodes with a temporal-efficiency filter and
searches it for subgoals; TTGS \citep{ttgs} and ALPS \citep{alps} do the same at test time for
frozen OGBench policies \citep{ogbench}. All of them execute subgoals with a learned policy (or a
state-space model with a behaviour prior). We take GAS's graph construction essentially unchanged
and give the subgoal to a pixel world model's CEM instead.

\paragraph{Value functions inside MPC.}
POLO \citep{polo}, TD-MPC \citep{tdmpc,tdmpc2} and Director \citep{director} put a learned value
at the end of a short model rollout; goal-conditioned terminal values have been used for
multi-task MPC \citep{gcterminal}. Those values are trained with rewards or with the model. Our
critic is a reachability classifier trained from hindsight and cross-episode negatives on the
offline data, and its contribution is a budget-aware hitting time rather than a value.

\paragraph{Frozen world model and CEM planner.}
LeWM \citep{lewm} consists of a ViT encoder $E$ mapping a $224\times224$ frame to a 192-d CLS
latent $\z$ and a predictor $P$ that maps three past latents and three action blocks to the next
latent, where an action block is five consecutive environment actions (frame-skip 5). Planning is
CEM over $K=5$ blocks (25 environment steps): 300 candidates, 30 iterations, 30 elites, each
candidate scored by the terminal cost $c_{\mathrm{L2}}=\|\hat\z_K-\z_g\|^2$ between the predicted
block-$K$ latent and the encoded goal frame. The optimum is executed open loop for 25 steps and
the planner is called again. We keep the encoder, the predictor and this planner fixed
throughout and change only the cost function that scores $(\z_0,\hat\z_{1..K},\z_g)$.

\paragraph{Each of these failures are fixed separately.}
With the planner fixed, terminal L2 fails in two distinct ways beyond one horizon, which we
analyze in \cref{sec:problem}: a procedural one, already absorbed by the baseline's 25-step
replanning cadence, and a structural one: L2 loses its ranking signal beyond one horizon because
the start--goal distance saturates at the random-pair scale. We retain raw L2 within one horizon, via the final switch of
\cref{sec:subgoal}, and beyond it substitute three quantities designed to stay informative at
range: TDR distance in place of raw position (\cref{sec:graph}), a graph that follows the shell
rather than cutting across it (\cref{sec:graph,sec:subgoal}), and a critic that estimates
reachability directly (\cref{sec:critic}). \Cref{fig:approach} provides an overview of our approach.

\subsection{Temporal distance representation and dataset graph}
\label{sec:graph}
Let $\mathcal{D}=\{(\z,a,\z')\}$ denote the offline dataset of consecutive-frame transitions,
encoded once by the frozen encoder.
We train a temporal distance representation $\psiT:\mathbb{R}^{192}\to\mathbb{R}^{32}$ by
expectile TD (HILP/GAS \citep{hilp,gas}): with value $V(\z,\z_g)=-\|\psiT(\z)-\psiT(\z_g)\|$, EMA
target $V^-$, discount $\gamma=0.99$, TD target $q=-1+\gamma\,V^-(\z',\z_g)$, and, for
$(\z,a,\z')\sim\mathcal{D}$, a goal $\z_g$ drawn from $\z$'s own trajectory at a
$\mathrm{Geom}(1-\gamma)$ offset with probability $0.625$ and uniformly from $\mathcal{D}$
otherwise,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TDR}}=\mathbb{E}_{(\z,a,\z')\sim\mathcal{D},\,\z_g}\big[\ell_{0.99}\big(q-V(\z,\z_g)\big)\big],
  \qquad \ell_\tau(u)=|\tau-\1_{u<0}|\,u^2,
  \label{eq:tdr}
\end{equation}
(we use HILP/GAS's target-stabilisation trick: computing $q$ over the minimum of two independent
networks). At convergence
$\|\psiT(\z)-\psiT(\z_g)\|$ approximates the discounted minimum
step count between $\z$ and $\z_g$; we map distance in TDR space to environment steps using
$u_k=\mathrm{median}_t\|\psiT(\z_t)-\psiT(\z_{t+k})\|$ over same-episode pairs $k$ steps apart,
$k\in\{1,\dots,50\}$.

The graph follows GAS. Let $F(\z_t)$ be the first later frame of $t$'s trajectory with
$\|\psiT(F(\z_t))-\psiT(\z_t)\|\ge\Htd$ (the episode's last frame if none), and define temporal
efficiency
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{TE}(\z_t)=\cos\angle\big(\psiT(F(\z_t))-\psiT(\z_t),\ \psiT(\z_{t+\Htd})-\psiT(\z_t)\big),
  \label{eq:te}
\end{equation}
the agreement between where a real $\Htd$-step transition moves and the direction $\psiT$ calls
optimal. Frames with $\mathrm{TE}<\theta_{\mathrm{TE}}=0.9$ are dropped; the remainder are
clustered sequentially in $\psiT$-space -- assign to the nearest existing centre if within
$\Htd/2$, else open a new one, then reset centres to member means -- giving nodes $v\in V_G$ with
medoid latent $c_v$. Nodes within $\Htd$ of each other are joined by an edge weighted by their
$\psiT$-distance, with $\Htd=u_{12}$. At planning time the goal is attached as a temporary node
linked to every node within $\max(\Htd,1.2\,d_{\min})$, and one Dijkstra pass gives the cost-to-go
$D_g[v]$ from every node. On Push-T (18{,}685 expert episodes, 2.34M frames) the graph has 20.5k
nodes; Reacher (10k episodes, 2.01M frames) needs 535; Cube (10k episodes, 2.01M frames) needs
16k. Construction is a
one-off cost per environment, reported with the rest of the pipeline's compute in
\cref{sec:analysis}.

\subsection{Subgoal one horizon ahead, and the final switch}
\label{sec:subgoal}
At each planning call with current latent $\z_0$, we select the start node
\begin{equation}
  v^\star=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{v\,:\,\|\psiT(\z_0)-c_v\|\le\Htd} D_g[v]+\|\psiT(\z_0)-c_v\|,
  \label{eq:startnode}
\end{equation}
and walk the shortest path from $v^\star$ toward the goal until the cumulative $\psiT$-distance
from $\z_0$ first reaches the lookahead $\lambda=u_{25}$, the calibrated distance of one planning
horizon; call the node reached $v_\lambda$. The planning criterion is the piecewise cost
\begin{equation}
  c(\hat\z_K)=
  \begin{cases}
    \|\hat\z_K-\z_g\|^2 & \text{if } \|\psiT(\z_0)-\psiT(\z_g)\|\le\lambda \quad\text{(\emph{final switch})}\\[2pt]
    \|\psiT(\hat\z_K)-c_{v_\lambda}\| =: c_{\mathrm{sub}}(\hat\z_K) & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:subgoal}
\end{equation}
Two choices here are settled empirically in \cref{sec:ablation} rather than assumed: the lookahead
is exactly one horizon (a shorter one leaves progress on the table, and tracking the intermediate
predicted latents $\hat\z_{1..K-1}$ -- the predictor's least reliable outputs -- is worse than
scoring $\hat\z_K$ alone), and the final switch matters because a cluster medoid's radius is
comparable to the success tolerance while $\psiT$ has less resolution near the goal than latent
L2, which itself remains discriminative rather than saturated at that range
(\cref{app:predictor-error}: true start-goal distance 17.1 at $H=25$, clear of the 19.6
random-pair floor, already 19.4 by $H=50$).

\subsection{Budget-clocked expected hitting time}
\label{sec:critic}
A reachability critic $V(\z,\z_g,h)\in[0,1]$, a five-member MLP ensemble over $(\z,\z_g,h)$ with
$h$ on a grid of multiples of 5 up to $h_{\max}=50$, estimates the probability that the goal is
reached from $\z$ within $h$ environment steps. Five label sources supervise it by binary
cross-entropy:
\begin{align}
  \text{(i) hindsight:}\quad & y=\1_{h\ge\tau}; \notag\\
  \text{(ii) cross-episode:}\quad & y=0\ \text{if}\ \|\Delta_{\mathrm{obj}}\|>\delta_{50}; \notag\\
  \text{(iii) imagined:}\quad & \text{predictor rollout under logged actions, labelled as in (i)};
    \notag\\
  \text{(iv) Bellman:}\quad & q=\max_{m\le M}\big(\bar V(\hat\z'_m,\z_g,h-5)-\kappa\,
    \bar\sigma(\hat\z'_m,\z_g,h-5)\big)_{[0,1]}; \notag\\
  \text{(v) monotonicity:}\quad & \max\big(0,\ V(\z,\z_g,h)-V(\z,\z_g,h+5)\big). \notag
\end{align}
Here $\tau$ is the first logged step satisfying the success predicate toward a later frame,
$\delta_{50}$ is the largest displacement the data itself makes in 50 steps (so (ii) labels an
object/joint change past that as unreachable), and (v) enters the loss as a hinge penalty. In
(iv), $\hat\z'_m=P(\z,a_m)$ rolls the frozen predictor forward under $M$ candidate action blocks
$a_m$ sampled around the logged action, and $\bar V,\bar\sigma$ are the mean and standard
deviation of an EMA target ensemble, clipped to $[0,1]$ before the max so that a single head's
optimism on an off-manifold state cannot dominate. Labels for (i) and the negative-sampling gate
in (ii) are read from the dataset's state column, not from pixels: although the critic's inputs at
inference are latents only, its supervision is state-dependent rather than pixel-only.
\Cref{sec:limitations} discusses what this costs.

Rather than the usual $-\log V$ penalty, we score an endpoint by how long the goal is expected to
take from it, capped at the budget that will remain once the plan being scored has been executed:
\begin{equation}
  \ET(\hat\z_K, \z_g)=5\sum_{h\in\{0,5,\dots,h_{\mathrm{rem}}\}}\big(1-V(\hat\z_K,\z_g,h)\big),
  \qquad h_{\mathrm{rem}}=\min\big(h_{\max},\,B-t-5K\big),
  \label{eq:et}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the episode budget and $t$ the current step, supplied by a \emph{plan clock} that
advances by one horizon with every CEM call. Without it, the critic would be queried at a fixed
$h$ regardless of steps already spent, understating reachability whenever the true remaining
budget is smaller -- most severely near the end of an episode; the plan clock instead asks, at
every call, ``reachable in the steps that are actually left.'' Feasible endpoints are then ordered
by how soon they arrive; infeasible ones sit at the cap. The final criterion standardises both
terms by median and interquartile range over the 300 CEM candidates of each environment (denoted
$\tilde{\cdot}$ ) and adds them,
\begin{equation}
  c=\widetilde{c_{\mathrm{sub}}}+\beta\,\widetilde{\ET},\qquad \beta=1,
  \label{eq:compose}
\end{equation}
with $c_{\mathrm{L2}}$ in place of $c_{\mathrm{sub}}$ in the final phase. \Cref{alg:main}
(\cref{app:algorithm}) summarises one planning call.

\paragraph{What is frozen}
The TDR (50k steps, batch 1024) and the critic (60k steps) are the only trained components;
parameter counts and training cost are reported with the rest of the pipeline's compute in
\cref{sec:analysis}. The encoder, predictor, CEM hyperparameters, and graph-construction rules are
LeWM's and GAS's, unchanged. Two scalars are environment specific, $\Htd=u_{12}$ and
$\lambda=u_{25}$, and both are
read off the TDR calibration table rather than tuned; $\beta=1$, $\theta_{\mathrm{TE}}=0.9$ and
$h_{\max}=50$ are shared by every environment and protocol.

\paragraph{Environments and data.}
Push-T \citep{lewm}: 18{,}685 expert episodes; success when agent and block position errors are
below 20\,px and block angle error below $\pi/9$. Reacher (dm\_control): 10{,}000 episodes of 201
frames; success when both joints are within 0.05\,rad of the goal configuration (raw difference,
no angle wrap; the dataset's shoulder angle is unwrapped over $\pm8$\,rad). OGBench Cube
\citep{ogbench}: 10{,}000 episodes of 201 frames; success when the cube's position is within
0.04\,m of its target (\texttt{CubeEnv.\_compute\_successes}, position only, no orientation or
effector-pose requirement). Frozen LeWM
checkpoints are the public ones for each environment. 

\paragraph{Protocols.}
For a same-episode offset $K$, the goal is the frame $K$ steps after the start on the same
trajectory and the budget is $2K$ steps. For the cross-episode protocol the start and goal are
uniformly random frames of two different episodes with no distance or reachability filter and a
budget of one full episode. We use a fixed set of 50 tasks per protocol for every
method and every seed. Each seed retrains the learned assets (TDR, graph, critic) and
reseeds CEM sampling, so reported standard deviations include both sources of variation;
L2 has no learned assets, and its seeds vary CEM sampling only.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\emph{L2} is LeWM's planner. \emph{TDR} scores endpoints by $\|\psiT(\hat\z_K)-\psiT(\z_g)\|$ with no
graph; \emph{CTG} uses the graph cost-to-go $\min_v\|\psiT(\hat\z_K)-c_v\|+D_g[v]$ as a terminal value
with no subgoal commitment. \emph{Random} takes a uniform action from the env's own action space
at every step (no model, no planning): the floor L2 and Ours must clear (\cref{tab:main}).

planner stops short of goals the critic has already judged reachable.

reaches 42.4\%. The graph therefore adds 8--12 points over the critic alone at offset 50, 100
and cross-episode, and the critic adds 8--25 points over the graph alone. Without the critic the
graph finds a route but the block drifts once the approach begins (median final error 125\,px
vs.\ 26\,px with the critic).

\textbf{ET versus $-\log V$.} The two critic costs tie at long range, but $-\log V$ (f) loses
11 points at offset 25. There the critic saturates (67\% of the CEM population is feasible,
versus 39--41\% at offset 100 and cross-episode), and per-population standardisation turns that
saturation into a full vote. The capped hitting time keeps ranking feasible candidates by how
soon they arrive. With ET, a single $\beta=1$ is best or within one standard deviation of the
best on every protocol (\cref{tab:ladder}).

same range (0.327 $\to$ 0.203 $\to$ 0.191). This is the justification for the switch to L2 within the final horizon
(\cref{eq:subgoal}): L2 is the better signal at short range, and the graph is preferred at longer horizons where L2's ranking fails.

\paragraph{Our failures are closer misses than L2's.} Loosening Push-T's success threshold
from its default 20\,px/20$^\circ$ to 25\,px/25$^\circ$ and 30\,px/30$^\circ$

advantage in \cref{tab:main} is not an artifact of the strict threshold disproportionately
penalizing Our near-misses -- when Ours fails, it still remains closer to the goal than L2 does.

under 3.5\% after the first window at both same-ep 100 and cross-episode: nearly all of L2's
successes come from its first two plans, and additional budget shows marginal improvement
(cross-episode success rises only from 12.0\% at 100 steps to 14.4\% at 250).
Our hazard rate instead stays between 8.6\% and 18.3\% through same-ep 100's entire budget and
cross-episode's first three windows, so a substantial share of the tasks still unsolved keep
getting solved by later replans. This is explains how Our advantage in \cref{tab:main}
accumulates: it is able to maintain sustained progress at later steps, while L2 is incapable of doing so.

\section{Why terminal L2 fails beyond one horizon}
\label{sec:problem}
% \keypoints{
% \item Two failure causes: (a) procedural -- wide open-loop commitments compound predictor error
%       (91/34/6\% vs 91/41/13\%); (b) structural -- even replanned every 25 steps, terminal L2's
%       own ranking collapses.
% \item L2 stays reliable at offset 25 -- the regime the final switch relies on.
% \item Cross-episode is budget-limited: L2 gains almost nothing after its first replans.
% }
The L2 rows of \cref{tab:main} fall from 91.2\% at offset 25 to 40.8\% at 50, 13.2\% at 100 and
14.4\% cross-episode. We separate two causes.

\paragraph{Long open-loop rollouts compound predictor error.}
Widening LeWM's default $2\times25$-step rollouts naively to $2\times H$-step rollouts for longer offsets
lowers L2's success from 91.2\% at offset 25 to 34.4\% at offset 50 and 5.6\% at offset 100
(\cref{app:predictor-error}). The predictor's relative rollout error grows from 14.1\% of the start--goal
distance at $H=25$ to 39.8\% at $H=100$. This failure is procedural: replanning every 25 steps minimizes accumulated predictor error, and every L2 result in this paper uses that cadence.

\paragraph{The terminal distance saturates.}
Replanning does not rescue the cost itself. In LeWM's Push-T latent, two unrelated frames sit
19.6 apart on average. True same-episode start--goal distance is 17.1 at $H=25$ but already
19.4 at $H=50$ and 19.6 at $H=100$ (\cref{app:predictor-error}): beyond one horizon, the goal is
as far in L2 as a random frame, so terminal distance carries little information about which
candidate is closer in time. This is consistent with the degradation of correlation between the L2 scores of candidates against the oracle observed in \cref{sec:analysis} .

\paragraph{L2 measures distance through a region the data does not occupy.}
The saturation has a geometric cause. \cref{app:gaussian-shell} shows that, in high dimensions, an isotropic Gaussian puts almost all
its mass on a thin shell of radius $\sigma\sqrt d$ ($=13.88$) around the origin. \cref{fig:gaussian-shell-r-hist} verifies this for LeWM's Push-T
latents. Two points on a shell are joined by a chord
through its empty interior, while real trajectories stay on the shell. \cref{fig:gaussian-shell-interp} shows that for same-episode
pairs 100 steps apart, the
straight-line midpoint $\tfrac12(z_{\mathrm{start}}+z_{\mathrm{goal}})$ lies at radius 10.03, and
96.4\% of them fall below the shell's 1st percentile. Terminal L2
optimizes along this chord: it measures distance through states the data never visits, not the distance
the dynamics must travel along the shell. The TDR and the graph (\cref{sec:graph}) are built to
follow the data instead.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% \keypoints{
% \item Say the four things a reviewer will say first, in our words: privileged critic labels; goal
%       frames are in the graph; one frozen model family; SAGE is stronger on same-episode long
%       offsets.
% \item Each with the mitigation that exists or the experiment that would settle it.
% }
All results use one frozen model
family; whether the same graph and critic help DINO-WM or PLDM is untested. SAGE \citep{sage}
reports higher same-episode success than ours at long offsets with the same frozen model by
training a subgoal generator on 400k expert windows; we do not claim the same-episode state of the
art, and we expect a generator trained on single-trajectory windows to be unable to stitch, and thus not generalize adequately to cross-episode. Reacher's short-range loss (six points at
offset 25) is inside CEM noise over ten seeds but consistent in sign. All data are expert
demonstrations; the suboptimal-data regime where graph stitching is usually studied is not
covered.

\section{Conclusion}
A frozen pixel world model's planner fails past one horizon because its terminal cost cannot tell
which candidates can still reach the goal. Giving it a data-supported target one horizon along a
shortest path over the dataset, a budget-aware hitting-time estimate of what remains, and its own
objective for the last horizon repairs mid-range, long-range and cross-episode goal reaching with
nothing in the model or the planner retrained. The three pieces fix three different failures, and
the per-pair diagnostics say why the last one is necessary: the graph knows a route for every
cross-episode pair, and the planner cannot execute it without a reachability term.

\section{Planning algorithm}
\label{app:algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{One planning call of graph-assisted latent MPC}
\label{alg:main}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE frozen $E,P$; CEM; TDR $\psiT$; graph $(V_G,c_v,D_g)$; critic $V$; budget $B$; step $t$
\STATE $\z_0\gets E(o_t)$; \ $h_{\mathrm{rem}}\gets\min(h_{\max},B-t-5K)$
\IF{$\|\psiT(\z_0)-\psiT(\z_g)\|\le\lambda$} \STATE target $\gets$ goal; base cost $\gets c_{\mathrm{L2}}$
\ELSE \STATE $v^\star\gets\arg\min_{v:\|\psiT(\z_0)-c_v\|\le\Htd} D_g[v]+\|\psiT(\z_0)-c_v\|$
\STATE walk the shortest path from $v^\star$ until cumulative TDR distance $\ge\lambda$; target $\gets v_\lambda$; base cost $\gets c_{\mathrm{sub}}$
\ENDIF
\FOR{each CEM iteration}
  \STATE sample 300 action-block sequences; $\hat\z_{1..K}\gets P(\cdot)$
  \STATE $c\gets\widetilde{\text{base}}(\hat\z_K)+\widetilde{\ET}(\hat\z_K, z_g)$ with $\ET$ from \cref{eq:et}
  \STATE refit the CEM Gaussian on the 30 lowest-$c$ candidates
\ENDFOR
\STATE execute the mean sequence for $5K$ steps; $t\gets t+5K$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Live rendering must match the dataset}
\label{app:render}
The frozen encoder is sensitive to renderer versions. Reacher frames rendered with MuJoCo 3.12
lack the floor checker texture of the dataset's MuJoCo 3.5 frames; the pixel difference is 18/255
on average and moves the latent by a median of 15 units, about one 25-step gap, so the terminal
cost never reaches zero even with the arm at the goal. This alone took LeWM's own planner from 87\%
to 65\% at offset 25. Our launcher refuses to evaluate unless three dataset frames re-render to
within 4/255 of the stored pixels; Push-T (pygame) and Cube pass with 0.5/255 and $\le0.2/255$.


\section{Where the latent-L2 objective fails}
\label{app:lewm-failure}
This section traces one failure mode end to end: empirically, in how far apart start/goal
pairs actually sit in latent space as the horizon grows (\cref{app:predictor-error}); and
mechanistically, in why a straight line between two such points leaves the region the encoder
actually populates (\cref{app:gaussian-shell}). \Cref{app:cem-trace} follows with a separate,
operational check of the same failure inside CEM's own candidate ranking.

\subsection{Predictor rollout error by horizon}
\label{app:predictor-error}
Rolling the frozen predictor forward under real logged actions and comparing to the true
encoded endpoint, Push-T's latent rollout error grows with horizon but stays well below the
scale of two unrelated frames (mean 19.6) even at $H=100$ (\cref{tab:predictor-error-and-task-distance,fig:predictor-error-by-horizon}):
the predictor degrades gracefully rather than collapsing to an uninformative guess. This
matches theory: \cref{app:gaussian-shell}'s isotropic-Gaussian fit to the latent cloud
($d=192$, empirical $\|z-\mu\|$ mean 13.88) predicts a random-pair distance of
$\sqrt2\times13.88\approx19.63$, since $z_1-z_2\sim\mathcal N(0,2\sigma^2 I_d)$ for two
independent draws -- essentially exact agreement with the measured 19.6.

\paragraph{Task difficulty: how far apart are the actual start/goal pairs?} The random-pair
reference above (mean 19.6 on the 3{,}000-sample draw used for
\cref{fig:predictor-error-by-horizon}) is the scale at which two frames carry no information
about each other. Sampling same-episode (start, start$+H$) pairs the same way
\cref{fig:predictor-error-by-horizon} samples its rollout starts, but reading off the true
encoded distance $\|z_{\mathrm{start}}-z_{\mathrm{start}+H}\|$ instead of rolling the predictor
forward (\cref{tab:predictor-error-and-task-distance}), shows $H=5$ pairs sit far below the
random-pair scale (median 5.3, std 2.8) while $H=25$ is already most of the way there (17.1) and
$H=50$/$H=100$ are close to indistinguishable from it (19.4, 19.6) -- consistent with
\cref{sec:problem}'s Spearman collapse past $H{=}50$: an arbitrarily sampled goal at that range is
close to latent-distance-indistinguishable from a random unrelated frame, exactly the regime
where a cost that only reads terminal $z$-space L2 has nothing left to discriminate on. Relative
to that task scale, the predictor's own rollout error is already 18.9\% of the gap at $H=5$ and
grows to 39.8\% by $H=100$ -- a sizeable and growing fraction of the very distance CEM is trying
to cross, consistent with \cref{tab:main}'s OUR-vs-L2 gap widening at longer horizons. The
task200u pool used for evaluation agrees closely with this same-episode sampling wherever the two
overlap (same-ep 25/50/100 medians 17.2/19.6/19.5, std 4.1/3.7/2.1), so the two measurements of
task difficulty are not an artifact of either sampling scheme.

\subsection{Why a straight latent line goes out of distribution}
\label{app:gaussian-shell}
\Cref{app:predictor-error} shows the predictor itself degrades gracefully with horizon; this
section asks why the terminal L2 objective built on top of it still fails past $H{\approx}50$
(elite-ranking Spearman 0.396 $\to$ 0.086, \cref{sec:problem}). In a high-dimensional isotropic Gaussian,
essentially all probability mass concentrates on a thin shell at radius $\sigma\sqrt{d}$ rather
than near the mean, because the volume element $r^{d-1}\,dr$ grows faster than the Gaussian
density falls off until $r\approx\sigma\sqrt{d}$.\footnote{\url{https://aseemrb.me/blog/high-dimensional-gaussians-on-a-sphere/}}
If LeWM's Push-T latent cloud approximates such a shell, a straight line drawn between two points
\emph{on} the shell passes through the ball's interior, which the shell distribution assigns
almost no mass -- exactly the regime a $z$-space-L2 CEM plan implicitly cuts through when it
scores a candidate purely by terminal distance to a goal many steps away.

\paragraph{The latent cloud is close to an isotropic shell.} Fitting an isotropic Gaussian
$\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2 I_d)$ to all 2{,}336{,}736 encoded Push-T frames ($d=192$, $\mu$ and
$\sigma^2=\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)/d$ from the empirical mean and covariance) predicts $\|z-\mu\|$
should follow a $\chi_d(\sigma)$ distribution with mean 13.88. The true encoded frames match this
almost exactly (empirical mean 13.88, \cref{fig:gaussian-shell-r-hist}), and the participation
ratio $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)^2/\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma^2)=188.5$ out of 192 dimensions (98\%) confirms
the covariance itself is close to isotropic, so the $\chi_d$ comparison is not being flattered by
a degenerate few dominant directions.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{fig_gaussian_shell_r_hist}
  \caption{Push-T: actual $\|z-\mu\|$ over all 2.3M encoded frames vs.\ the $\chi_{192}$
  prediction from an isotropic Gaussian fit to the same mean/covariance.}
  \label{fig:gaussian-shell-r-hist}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{A straight-line midpoint is far off the shell; the real trajectory is not.} For 200{,}000
same-episode frame pairs 100 steps apart (the same-ep-100 horizon), both endpoints sit on the
shell as expected (start mean 14.15, goal mean 13.60), and so does the actual logged frame at the
trajectory's true midpoint (mean 13.81). The linear interpolation
$\tfrac12(z_{\mathrm{start}}+z_{\mathrm{goal}})$ does not: its mean radius is 10.03, 5.42
shell-$\sigma$ below the shell mean, and 96.4\% of the 200{,}000 interpolated midpoints fall below
the shell's own 1st percentile radius (\cref{fig:gaussian-shell-interp} in \cref{sec:problem}). The two populations do
not overlap. This isolates the effect to interpolation itself, not to trajectory midpoints being
unusual in general: the real dynamics stay on the shell, curving around it rather than cutting
through its interior, which is exactly the structure a graph built in a temporal-distance
representation is meant to follow instead of a raw latent straight line
(\cref{sec:method,sec:main}).

\paragraph{Scope.} This diagnostic is Push-T-only and tests the naive linear interpolation, not
the predictor's own imagined rollout under the $z$-space-L2 objective CEM actually optimizes;
whether the predictor's rollout exhibits the same off-shell dip, and whether the effect holds on
Reacher and Cube, is not yet measured.
\emph{Scripts: \texttt{scripts/gas\_mpc\_gaussian\_shell\_diag.py} and
\texttt{scripts/gas\_mpc\_gaussian\_shell\_interp\_diag.py}.}
